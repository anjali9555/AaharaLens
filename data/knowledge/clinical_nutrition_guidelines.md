# Clinical Nutrition & Dietary Guidelines

## Macronutrient Optimization & Energy Balance
- **Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE)**: Derived from Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) multiplied by the Physical Activity Level (PAL). BMR is estimated via the Mifflin-St Jeor formula, validated as the most accurate non-calorimetric clinical formula.
- **Protein Leverage Hypothesis**: Humans prioritize protein intake. If meals are protein-deficient, individuals tend to overconsume carbohydrates and fats until protein requirements are met. Ensuring 25-30g of protein per main meal induces profound satiety via peptide YY (PYY) and GLP-1 hormone release.
- **Target Protein Recommendations**:
  - General Sedentary Population: 0.8g - 1.0g per kg bodyweight.
  - Active / Endurance Athletes: 1.2g - 1.6g per kg bodyweight.
  - Hypertrophy / Resistance Training: 1.6g - 2.2g per kg bodyweight.
  - Caloric Deficit / Fat Loss: 1.8g - 2.4g per kg to spare lean muscle mass.

## Glycemic Management & Diabetes Care
- **Glycemic Index (GI)**: Measures the rate at which 50g of carbohydrates from a food elevates blood glucose compared to pure glucose (GI 100).
  - Low GI (< 55): Moong dal, lentils, oats, apples, raw paneer, chana.
  - High GI (> 70): Refined white bread, cornflakes, puffed rice, instant mashed potatoes.
- **Glycemic Load (GL)**: Formula: `(GI * Available Carbohydrates) / 100`. A food with high GI but small carbohydrate portion (e.g., watermelon) has a low GL.
- **Nutritional Buffering**: Adding dietary fiber, healthy fats, or protein to a carbohydrate meal blunts the insulin spike by slowing gastric emptying. Example: Adding cucumber salad and dal to steamed white rice drops the postprandial glucose spike significantly.

## Food Synergy and Bioavailability
- **Complementary Proteins**: Plant proteins are often limited in one or more essential amino acids. Cereals (wheat, rice) are limited in lysine but rich in methionine. Pulses (dal, beans) are rich in lysine but limited in methionine. Combining Dal + Rice or Roti + Dal provides a 100% complete amino acid score (PDCAAS = 1.0).
- **Iron Absorption Synergy**: Plant-based (non-heme) iron is in the ferric state (Fe3+). Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) reduces it to ferrous iron (Fe2+), boosting intestinal absorption by up to 300%. Squeezing fresh lemon over spinach or dal optimizes iron uptake.
