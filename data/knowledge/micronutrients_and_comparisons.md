# Micronutrients, Superfoods & Food Comparisons

## Paneer vs Tofu: Detailed Clinical Comparison
- **Paneer (Dairy Cottage Cheese)**:
  - Calories (per 100g): ~265 kcal
  - Protein: ~18.3g (Complete animal protein, rich in leucine and casein)
  - Fat: ~20.8g (Predominantly saturated dairy fats)
  - Calcium: ~480mg (High bioavailability)
  - Carbohydrates: ~3.4g
  - Best for: Muscle hypertrophy, strength athletes, keto diet, children requiring dense energy and bone development.
- **Tofu (Soy Bean Curd)**:
  - Calories (per 100g): ~76 kcal
  - Protein: ~8.2g (Complete plant protein, soy isoflavones)
  - Fat: ~4.8g (Primarily heart-healthy polyunsaturated fatty acids)
  - Calcium: ~350mg (if calcium-set)
  - Carbohydrates: ~1.9g
  - Cholesterol: 0 mg (100% plant-based)
  - Best for: Weight loss (delivers high protein for 1/3 the calories of paneer), cardiovascular health, reducing LDL cholesterol, and vegan lifestyles.

## Oats: Comprehensive Health Benefits
- **Beta-Glucan Soluble Fiber**: Forms a viscous gel in the digestive tract that binds to bile acids (made of cholesterol) and excretes them, forcing the liver to pull LDL cholesterol from the bloodstream. Consuming 3g of beta-glucan daily reduces total cholesterol by 5-10%.
- **Avenanthramides**: Unique polyphenolic antioxidants found almost exclusively in oats. They increase nitric oxide production in endothelium cells, dilating blood vessels and lowering systolic blood pressure.
- **Satiety & Gut Microbiota**: Promotes growth of beneficial Bifidobacteria and increases gut production of short-chain fatty acids (acetate, propionate, butyrate).

## Critical Micronutrient Considerations in Vegetarian Diets
- **Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)**: Absent in non-animal plant foods. Essential for nerve myelination and red blood cell formation. Vegetarians must consume fortified foods, curd/milk, or an oral methylcobalamin supplement.
- **Vitamin D3 (Cholecalciferol)**: Regulates over 200 genes, immune response, and calcium absorption. Most synthesized via sunlight on skin (UVB).
- **Omega-3 Fatty Acids**: Plant sources (flaxseeds, chia, walnuts) provide Alpha-Linolenic Acid (ALA), which the human body converts to EPA/DHA at a low rate (< 5-8%). Algal oil supplements or concentrated seeds are recommended for brain and eye health.
